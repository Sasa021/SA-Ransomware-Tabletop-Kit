# Participant handbook

Exercise Wharf Lantern. One sitting, about 2 to 3 hours. You play a role at a fictional Cape Town firm while it deals with ransomware. You are not being scored. You are being asked to decide, in order, with incomplete facts.

**Not legal advice.** The notification guide cited during the exercise explains published duties and tells you where the law is unsettled. It is a discussion aid. It is not an opinion you can hand to a client.

## What you are walking into

Harbourline Discretionary (Pty) Ltd is a fictional discretionary financial services provider on the Foreshore. Read [scenario/company-brief.md](scenario/company-brief.md) before you arrive. That brief is the whole of what you know at the start. Later injects add facts. They never ask you to invent clients, amounts, or technical detail.

The story runs from Monday 07:40 to Friday morning. Your real-world clock is shorter. When you say "in an hour", say whether you mean an hour in the story or an hour in the room.

## How the time will feel

The facilitator reads a short inject. You restate the new facts. You decide. The scribe reads the decision back. Then the next inject arrives, and it will not undo the decision for you. If you left the network up, the story continues with the network up.

You will feel pressure to be clever about the attack. The exercise is not asking for that. It is asking what the firm does about payment, the police, backups, and the people whose information and money instructions are caught in the outage.

## Rules

1. Stay inside the brief and the injects. If it was not read out, the firm does not know it.
2. "We do not know yet" is a useful answer. Write it down when you use it.
3. You may disagree. Ask the scribe to record the disagreement, then let the incident lead choose a working path.
4. Do not use a real person's story, a real employer's incident, or a real criminal site as colour.
5. Do not draft, fetch, or describe attack procedures, payment routes, or decryptors. The facilitator will stop that talk.
6. Be careful with names of clients. The injects do not give you a client name. Do not create one.
7. You may pause if the subject is too close to a real event. You do not have to explain.

## Roles

The facilitator assigns these at the start. If you hold two, say which hat you are wearing before you speak.

| Role | What you owe the room |
| --- | --- |
| Incident lead | A decision, or a conscious deferral with an owner and a time |
| Scribe | A log of facts, decisions, dissent, and open questions. Not a transcript of jokes |
| Information Officer | The POPIA question: reasonable grounds, who is told, and whether any delay is even available |
| Technical lead | What BaySwitch has confirmed, what is suspected, and what you will isolate |
| Communications lead | Words that match the facts, for staff, clients, the board, and the press |
| Client services | The human deadline, including the property transfer and the missed calls |
| BaySwitch | The operator's view. You notify the firm. You do not notify the Regulator on Harbourline's behalf |

The board chair, Dr Naledi September, is not played unless the facilitator asks someone to take a two-minute phone call. She is the person you brief, not the person who runs the incident from another suburb.

## What good looks like

- You can say what you know, what you suspect, and what you are refusing to guess.
- You can name which duty you are talking about: POPIA section 22, Joint Standard 2 of 2024, or Cybercrimes Act section 54.
- You can leave a decision you dislike clearly recorded.
- You can write a sentence to a client that you would still send after the next inject.

## What to bring

A pen, if the scribe log is on paper. A copy of the company brief. Questions. You do not need a laptop. If you bring one, keep it closed unless the facilitator asks the communications lead to type a holding statement.

## After you leave

The facilitator owns the write-up. If you are quoted in the repository, you will be asked first. You may send a correction to the scribe log on the same day if something was recorded wrongly. Do not fill in, a week later, a decision the room never made.
