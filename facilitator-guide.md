# Facilitator guide

You are running Exercise Wharf Lantern for Harbourline Discretionary (Pty) Ltd. The firm is fictional. The decisions are not supplied. Your job is to keep time, keep the facts stable, and keep the room honest about what it does not know.

Read this guide, the company brief, and all eight injects before the day. Read [human-factors.md](human-factors.md) as well. The psychology of the room is part of the exercise, not a side note.

**Not legal advice.** When the room argues about a deadline, open [regulatory/breach-notification-guide.md](regulatory/breach-notification-guide.md) and read the citation. If the guide says to verify a commencement date or a template cover page, do not let a confident participant replace that check with a number they remember from a course.

## What you are facilitating

A 2 to 3 hour discussion. People stay in role, hear one inject at a time, and make decisions a small Cape Town FSP could make on the day. They do not investigate a live network, draft malware, or learn how to pay a criminal.

A good sitting ends with a scribe log that shows:

- What was decided
- Who owns it
- What was left unknown
- Where the room disagreed

A weak sitting ends with a story about "what hackers do" and no owner for the client notice.

## Room rules you say at the start

Say these in your own voice. The ideas are:

1. Harbourline is fictional. Do not import a real client or a real employer.
2. Missing facts stay missing. "We do not know yet" is a complete sentence.
3. Disagree on the record. The scribe writes the dissent. Nobody is marked down for it.
4. Do not look up criminal marketplaces, payment methods, or leak sites. The facilitator will stop that search.
5. The exercise can still feel heavy. Anyone may pause the room. A pause is not a failure.
6. Phones are down except the scribe's timer and a shared clock.

## Clock

Use the table in the README. Start a visible timer.

- If a debate is useful and on the decision, let it run and steal time from inject 8.
- If a debate is about a fact you did not give, say "That fact is not in the brief" and move.
- At minute 12 of a 15 minute inject, ask "What are we deciding, and who owns it?"
- Take the break even if the room wants to push on, unless you are on the 2 hour version. Tired people start inventing evidence.

Short version, about 2 hours: 10 minute open, no break, 10 minutes each for injects 1, 2, 3, and 7, 15 minutes each for injects 4, 5, and 6, 10 minute close.

## How to release an inject

1. Read the scenario time, then the aloud section, slowly.
2. Ask one person to restate the new facts in two sentences. Correct only clear mistakes.
3. Ask the questions for that inject from [decision-points.md](decision-points.md). You do not need every question. You do need one question on the decision the inject is built for.
4. If the room is silent, use the prompts below. Do not answer your own prompt.
5. Before you move on, the scribe reads the decision back. If there is no decision, the scribe writes "no decision" and the reason.
6. Then, if you are using the map, show the technique rows for that inject. Ask which status the room agrees with, seen or suspected.

## If the room goes quiet

- "What would you do in the next 30 minutes of scenario time, and what would you refuse to do?"
- "Who is not in the room and still needs to hear this?"
- "What are you assuming that the inject did not say?"

## If the room splits

- Give each side one minute. Scribe records both. The incident lead must pick a working decision or explicitly defer it, with an owner and a scenario time to revisit. A deferred decision is still a decision.

## If someone starts teaching an attack

Stop it. Say: "We are the firm. We do not need the procedure. We need the decision this fact forces." Point at the MITRE row only after the discussion, and only at the level of the name and the observation.

If someone proposes paying and then explains wallets, coins, or contact methods, stop the method. The allowed conversation is whether a mandated person will even ask the bank and counsel to consider a payment. The kit has no payment path because a portfolio exercise should not become one.

## If someone gets distressed

Ransomware stories sit close to real work and real family finances. Watch for a person who goes quiet after having been active, who laughs the content off in a flat way, or who starts describing their own workplace in detail.

- Pause. Say the scenario can wait.
- Offer a break and a glass of water. Offer them a different role, or the door, with no explanation required.
- Do not ask them to share the real incident.
- If you are in a classroom or a workplace, use that organisation's support route. This guide does not invent a counselling number.
- At the close, ask: "Is anyone leaving with a worry about a real incident at work or at home? If yes, tell me before we pack up, or message me today. You do not have to tell the whole room."

You are not their clinician. You are the person who will not push past a stop signal. [human-factors.md](human-factors.md) has the longer note on psychological safety. Use it.

## Inject notes

These notes are for you. They are not model answers. A room that chooses differently, and writes why, has still done the exercise.

### Inject 1

Watch for the WhatsApp photo. Ask whether the picture is already an uncontrolled disclosure of the incident, and who will send the internal holding line. Do not let the room "wipe" machines. They have not been given a forensic plan, and wiping is how logs disappear.

### Inject 2

The Thursday attachment is a hypothesis. If the room treats it as proven initial access, ask what would change if the attachment is unrelated. The Stellenbosch laptop is the containment question: it is outside the office and still connected.

### Inject 3

BaySwitch is waiting for written permission to disable administrator accounts. That pause is the point. Also make the board chair real: the lead owes Dr September a call with facts, not a promise that clients will never hear.

### Inject 4

This is the pay or not pay inject. Keep the discussion on authority, purpose, and residual harm. There is no sample file and no price. If the room invents a price, strike it from the log. Johan must not be allowed to promise clients that payment deletes stolen copies. It does not, on the facts you have, and you will not add a fact that says it does.

### Inject 5

Put three columns on a board: POPIA, Joint Standard, Cybercrimes Act. If a proposed action does not name the column, it is not ready. Correct the "no data was stolen" sentence if it appears. The lawful delay for telling data subjects is narrow. Read section 22(3) rather than paraphrasing from memory.

### Inject 6

Make the communications lead draft from the templates, live, for five minutes. Then have a second person try to break any sentence that goes beyond the facts. The property transfer is the pressure. The room may tell the attorney that the balance system is down. It may not invent a balance.

### Inject 7

Force a choice between option A, option B as an offline inspection, or "neither until a named test passes". Option C is not available. Do not reward a payment decision from inject 4 with a magical recovery. Friday's report is the consequence of the recovery point they pick.

### Inject 8 and the hot wash

Read the decisions back. Then ask only these:

- Which decision was fastest, and which was avoided?
- Where did stress or hierarchy change what was said?
- Which sentence to clients would you still send?
- What will you write in the after action report as an open question, with no invented answer?

Stop on time. Social chat can happen after the check about real incidents.

## After the sitting

Same day, while the handwriting is still readable:

1. Photograph or scan the scribe log and any board you want to keep. Follow [docs/screenshot-checklist.md](docs/screenshot-checklist.md).
2. File those notes as [evidence/README.md](evidence/README.md) describes. Do not commit faces or phone numbers without consent.
3. Draft [templates/after-action-report.md](templates/after-action-report.md) from the log. Quote the room. Do not improve their decisions in the draft.
4. Fill **Results and lessons learned** in the README with the same material. If a line did not happen, leave it blank.

## Solo rehearsal

You can walk the injects alone to test the timing. Mark the scribe log "rehearsal, one person". Do not copy that log into the results section and present it as a facilitated exercise. The portfolio claim is that you ran the room.
