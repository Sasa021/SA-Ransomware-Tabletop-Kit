# Crisis communications plan

For Exercise Wharf Lantern at Harbourline Discretionary (Pty) Ltd, a fictional firm. The templates are empty of outcomes on purpose. Fill a bracket only with a fact from the company brief or from an inject the facilitator has already read. If the bracket is still unknown, write "we do not yet know" or delete the sentence.

**Not legal advice, and not a press release you can send for a real firm.** A real notice to data subjects has to meet POPIA section 22, including the content in section 22(5). A real notice to financial customers also has to sit beside Joint Standard 2 of 2024 paragraph 7.4.1(e), which expects a clear communication strategy for customers affected by a cyber incident, including any recourse available to them. Counsel and the Information Officer sign a real notice. In this exercise, the communications lead drafts and the incident lead accepts the draft on the record.

This plan is where a public relations brief and an incident brief meet. The skill is not a clever line. The skill is matching the words to the facts, picking a channel that reaches the person, and saying who is allowed to speak.

## Rules for every draft

1. Separate what has happened, what you are doing, and what you are asking the reader to do.
2. Do not say personal information was not taken. The story does not prove that.
3. Do not name a criminal group, a ransom amount, a payment, or a leak site. The injects do not give you those facts.
4. Do not name a client. Do not use the staff WhatsApp group as a publishing channel.
5. One voice to people outside the firm. In the story that voice is Amina Fourie, unless she delegates in the scribe log. Everyone else declines comment and passes the query on.
6. Staff hear the news from the firm before they hear it from a journalist. If that sequence has already failed, say so internally and correct it. Do not pretend it held.
7. Afrikaans and isiXhosa are widely used by clients in the Western Cape. The brief does not give you a translated notice. If the room decides a translation is required, record who will produce it and that it must match the English text. Do not paste a machine translation into the after action report and treat it as approved.

## Holding statements

A holding statement buys a clean hour. It is not the section 22 notice. Label it as a holding statement when you use it.

### Phone, for client services

> Harbourline is dealing with a systems incident at our Foreshore office. Our portfolio system is unavailable, so I cannot confirm a balance or release a payment from it today. I will not guess a figure. We are working through what happened, including whether client information was affected. If you need to take a protective step with your bank, do that with your bank. I will call you again by [time you can actually meet]. I am [name], and the person accountable for what we tell clients is [name].

### E-mail, for a client who writes in

Subject: Harbourline systems incident, what we know on [day]

> We are writing to every client because our file server and our portfolio system are not available. We know files on the office file server cannot be opened, and a message on some screens claims those files were encrypted. We do not yet know whether copies of client information were taken. We are not going to tell you "no data was taken" while that is still unknown.
>
> What we are doing: [one sentence on isolation or account changes that the room has actually ordered]. What we are not doing: we are not asking you for a password, a one-time pin, or a payment to Harbourline by reply to this e-mail.
>
> What you can do: treat unexpected calls about your Harbourline account with caution, and speak to your bank if you are worried about the account number we hold. We will write again by [date] or sooner if we learn that personal information was accessed.
>
> [Name, role, phone]

### Board chair, five lines

> 1. What happened: [one fact].
> 2. What is down: [systems].
> 3. Decisions taken: [payment position, isolation, who speaks].
> 4. Who we are notifying: [list only those the room has agreed to notify].
> 5. Next update: [scenario time].

## Internal staff message

Send this on e-mail. Repeat the operational points in person. Do not send it on the WhatsApp group that still includes former contractors. Tell someone to remove people who do not need to be there, and record that as an action.

Subject: Incident today, what to do, what not to say

> Team
>
> We have an incident. Files on the office file server are not opening, and a note on some computers claims they were encrypted. LedgerQuay and the client portal are [up / down, as at the inject you are in]. This e-mail is the update. Side groups are how rumours leave the building. Please do not add detail, client names, or screenshots to WhatsApp.
>
> Until [name] says otherwise:
>
> - Do not switch machines on or off unless [name] has asked you to. Do not delete files to "tidy".
> - Do not pay any message that claims to be from IT or from a recovery firm. We will not ask you for a password on WhatsApp.
> - If a client, an attorney, or a journalist calls, take their number and tell them [name] will respond. You can say we have a systems incident and that you cannot confirm balances. You cannot say data was or was not stolen.
> - If you are with a client, as [the Stellenbosch laptop situation, if still live], follow the technical instruction you have already been given: [instruction].
> - If this is frightening, or if it reminds you of a real incident, tell [name] and step out. You do not have to perform calm.
>
> Next update at [time].
>
> [Name]

## Customer notice (section 22 shape)

Use this when the Information Officer has decided there are reasonable grounds under section 22, or when the room wants to see what a notice requires before it makes that decision. If the second, mark the top "DRAFT, grounds not yet accepted".

The four content blocks below are the section 22(5) list. Keep them visible. A warm introduction can sit above them. It cannot replace them.

Subject: Notice of a security compromise at Harbourline

> Harbourline Discretionary (Pty) Ltd is the responsible party for the personal information we hold about you. Our Information Officer is [name]. We are writing because [state the reasonable grounds in two sentences. Do not claim a cause you have not established].
>
> **What may have been involved.** Categories we hold, and that may be affected, are contact details, identity numbers, tax reference numbers, bank account numbers used for contributions and withdrawals, FICA documents, and beneficiary details. We do not yet know the list of people affected. We will update you if we narrow it or widen it. We are not aware of misuse of these details. Absence of a report of misuse is not the same as proof that nothing was taken.
>
> **Possible consequences.** If an unauthorised person has these details, they could attempt to impersonate you, to redirect a payment, or to open an account in your name. We do not have a report that this has happened to Harbourline clients.
>
> **What we are doing.** [Measures actually taken: isolation, passwords or administrator accounts disabled, regulator notification lodged or being lodged, SAPS report made or not, backups being tested. Nothing else.]
>
> **What we recommend you do.** Contact your bank if you are concerned about the account we use for your portfolio. Be cautious of calls or messages that use this incident to ask for a password or a one-time pin. You may ask us what information we hold. You may also contact the Information Regulator. Use the contact details published on https://inforegulator.org.za/ rather than a number copied from memory into this draft.
>
> **Who accessed the information.** We do not know the identity of the unauthorised person. We will not guess.
>
> We will write again by [date]. If you need us sooner, call [phone] and ask for [role].
>
> [Name], Information Officer, Harbourline Discretionary (Pty) Ltd, Cape Town

If the Regulator has directed a form of publicity under section 22(6), follow the direction and file it with the notice. The injects do not contain such a direction.

Paragraph 7.4.1(e) of Joint Standard 2 of 2024 also expects customers to hear what recourse they have. In this draft the recourse you can honestly offer is: how to reach the firm, how to reach the Regulator, and the suggestion to speak to their bank. Do not invent a compensation scheme. Harbourline has no cyber insurance policy in the brief.

## Media questions and answers

Spokesperson: one person, named in the scribe log. Everyone else: "I am not the right person. Please e-mail [address]."

Answer only what the left-hand column asks. The right-hand column is a pattern, not a script you have to read word for word.

| Question | Pattern you can defend |
| --- | --- |
| Have you been hit by ransomware? | We have an incident in which files on our office system cannot be opened and a message claims they were encrypted. We are treating it as a serious cyber incident. |
| Has client data been stolen? | We do not yet know. We are not saying it was, and we are not saying it was not. Clients will hear from us directly as soon as we have grounds to be more specific. |
| Should clients freeze their bank accounts? | We cannot instruct a client's bank. We have told clients to speak to their bank if they are worried, and to refuse any call that asks for a password or a one-time pin. |
| Will you pay? | We do not discuss payment speculation. [Add the room's actual position only if the incident lead has agreed it can be said in public. "No decision has been made" is acceptable. A strategy discussion is not.] |
| How did the attackers get in? | We do not know the entry path. We will not speculate on a person or an e-mail. |
| How many clients are affected? | About 1,400 clients are on our books. That is not the same as a count of people whose information was accessed. We do not have that count. |
| Are you talking to the regulators and the police? | [Say only what has been done. "We are assessing our duties to the Information Regulator, the Financial Sector Conduct Authority, and the police" is fair while inject 5 is open. Once a report is made, you may say a report was made. Do not invent a case number.] |
| A source says you are paying tonight. | That is not a statement from Harbourline. Staff chat is not a decision. |
| Will you give us the note, the sample, or a client file? | No. |
| Who is accountable? | The managing director is the incident lead and the Information Officer, unless we have told you otherwise in writing. |

If you do not know, say you do not know, and say when you will next be able to update. "No comment" is quieter and usually worse, because the journalist already has the WhatsApp version.

## After the exercise

File the drafts the room actually produced with the scribe log. If a draft was rejected, keep the rejected sentence in the after action report and say why it was cut. That rejected sentence is often the lesson. Do not replace it with a cleaner line nobody said.
